A rep with an hour to make calls wants the leads in order. `hotLeads` reads the leads and gives each a score from what the vendor reports about them: a positive interest label, replies, clicks, opens, and how recent the last reply is. It returns the leads highest score first.

The score is a weighted sum, and every part of it comes back with the lead. It is not a prediction: no model is trained or called, and nothing says a lead with 95 points is more likely to buy than one with 31. It says the first has a positive label and replied twice, and the second replied once. The weights are inputs, so you can change what "first" means.

## Before you start

- Node 22 or later.
- The code below runs on the in-memory adapter, so it needs no vendor key. Each sample on this page is a file in the repository that the test suite runs; the output shown is what it printed.
- For real data: an API key for a tool that reports per-lead counts. Today that is Instantly. Read "What each vendor can serve" below before you plan around Smartlead or HeyReach.

The score is the sum of five parts, with these defaults:

| Part | Points | Input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The vendor's interest label is a positive one | 50 | `weight_interest`, `positive_labels` |
| Each reply, up to `count_cap` | 10 | `weight_reply` |
| Each click, up to `count_cap` | 5 | `weight_click` |
| Each open, up to `count_cap` | 1 | `weight_open` |
| A reply received right now, fading evenly to 0 after `recency_days` | 20 | `weight_recency` |

`count_cap` is 5 and `recency_days` is 14. `positive_labels` defaults to `Interested`, `Meeting Booked` and `Meeting Completed`, matched without regard to case.

Read the output above against that table:

- **Ava** scores 95: 50 for the label, 20 for two replies, 15 because her last reply was 3.5 of 14 days ago, 5 for a click and 5 for opens. She has six opens; the cap counts five.
- **Cara** is third although the vendor labelled her `Not Interested`. A label that is not positive earns 0 points, and her reply and its recency still count. The ranking does not read what a reply says.
- **Dev** has 40 opens and 5 points. The cap is there so opens cannot outrank a reply.
- **A part that is `null` is unknown, not zero.** Ben has no label, so `parts.interest` is `null`. The function prints "unknown".

Who is left out:

- A lead with no label and no engagement data is not scored 0. It is left out and counted in `totals.withoutData`.
- Unsubscribed and bounced leads are left out and counted in `totals.excluded`.
- A lead whose known parts add up to 0 is not on the list.

The ranking needs per-lead reply, click and open counts (`leads.engagement`). Without them only the interest label and a `replied` status are left to score on.

- **Instantly** declares `leads.engagement` and can list every lead. It serves the full ranking.
- **Smartlead**'s adapter does not declare `leads.engagement`: the only per-lead counts are in an endpoint whose response is not documented, and in a CSV export. The call succeeds with a note, and scores rest on the label and the status.
- **HeyReach** reports no per-lead counts at all, and has no list of every lead, so the call without a campaign is refused. With a campaign ref it runs, but the adapter's notes say a campaign's leads carry no interest label either. On HeyReach this ranking has almost nothing to rank on. Do not build a call list on it.

## What it costs

One request per page of leads read. `max_leads` (default 500, at most 20,000) sets the ceiling, and a vendor request returns about 50 to 100 leads. On Smartlead a read without a campaign walks every campaign's leads, so pass `campaign`. `maxRequests` on the instance caps the whole call; past it the call fails with `RateLimited` instead of spending your vendor quota.

| What happened | What you get | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More leads than `max_leads` | `meta.complete` is `false` and a note says where it stopped. The ranking covers the leads read. | Raise `max_leads`, or rank one campaign. |
| `limit` is smaller than the number of hot leads | A shorter list. `totals.hot` still counts them all and `meta.complete` stays `true`. | Nothing. |
| No lead has a label or a count | An empty list, with the leads in `totals.withoutData` | Check that the vendor reports per-lead counts. |
| A negative weight, or another input out of range | `Invalid`, before any request | Fix the value. |
| The adapter cannot list every lead and no campaign was given | `Unsupported`, naming `leads.list` | Pass `campaign`. |
| The adapter reports no per-lead counts | An answer with a note. Count parts are `null`. | Treat the list as a list of labelled leads, not a ranking. |
| A rejected key | `Unauthorized`. It is not retried. | Check the key and its scope. |

## Limits

- The score orders leads. It does not estimate a chance of anything, and two scores are only comparable under the same weights.
- It reads the label and the counts, never the reply text. A lead who replied "stop emailing me" and was not marked unsubscribed still earns reply points.
- Ties are broken by the most recent reply, then by lead ref.
- One entry per lead record. A person in two campaigns appears twice, scored on each record's own counts.
- Open counts depend on tracking pixels, which mail clients load on their own. Keep `weight_open` low.
- On an `unverified` adapter, check a list against the vendor's own lead view before calling from it.
