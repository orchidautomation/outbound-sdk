Seven insights about the people in your campaigns. Each is a method on `outbound.insights` once the [insights plugin](/docs/insights) is added. What every answer carries (`data`, `meta.complete`, `meta.notes`) and how inputs are checked is on the [overview](/docs/insights).

People are matched by exact identifier only: an email address, lowercased, or a LinkedIn profile. Nothing is matched by name.

## The data the samples run on

Every sample on this page runs on the in-memory adapter with this data: one client on an email tool with three campaigns and on a LinkedIn tool with one. It runs at a fixed clock, 2026-11-14 12:00 UTC. See [how the samples are run](/docs/samples).

## `personSummary`

Everything about one person across campaigns and channels: each campaign they are in, how many messages they were sent, whether and how often they replied, which steps they replied to, and their interest label.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `person` | required | at least 3 characters | An email address or a LinkedIn profile URL. Anything else is `Invalid`. |
| `max_campaigns` | 20 | 1 to 50 | The most campaigns to report on. |

`data` (`PersonSummary`):

- `found`: `false` when no record matched.
- `identifiers`: every exact identifier the records carry, as `email:` and `linkedin:` keys.
- `campaigns`: one entry per campaign, with `campaignName`, `campaignStatus`, `channel`, `provider`, `lead`, `leadStatus`, `interest` (the vendor's own label), `messagesSent`, `replies`, `replied`, `repliedSteps` (counted from 0), `lastStep`, `firstContactedAt`, `lastContactedAt` and `lastRepliedAt`.
- `totals`: `campaigns`, `campaignsReplied`, `campaignReplyRate` (campaigns they replied in, divided by campaigns that sent them at least one message), `messagesSent`, `replies` and `byChannel`.

- **Needs:** `leads.lookup`, `leads.timeline`.
- **Costs:** one lookup per identifier, then up to 2 requests per campaign (the lead's timeline and the campaign).
- **Notes it can add:** a thread held fewer sent messages than the lead's last step, so messages sent is counted from the last step.
- **Does not:** match by name, or find a LinkedIn record from an email address unless some tool holds both for that person. `repliedSteps` is empty when the vendor ties a reply to no step.
- **Refuses:** with `Unsupported`, before any read, while the [`redact` plugin](/docs/plugins/redact) masks `email` or `linkedin`, as `outbound mcp` does by default. Masked records could not be matched, so it does not report a known person as not found.

## `accountSummary`

Everyone at one company, across campaigns and tools, by exact email domain.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `domain` | required | at least 3 characters | The company's domain, an email address at it, or a URL on it. |
| `allow_free_domains` | `false` | boolean | Accept an email provider domain such as `gmail.com`, which is not one company. |
| `max_leads` | 1,000 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to read in all. |
| `max_campaigns` | 20 | 1 to 200 | The most campaigns to read leads from, when the adapter cannot list every lead at once. |
| `positive_labels` | `Interested`, `Meeting Booked`, `Meeting Completed` | at least 1 character each | Vendor interest labels that count as interested, matched without regard to case. |

`data` (`AccountSummary`):

- `domain`: the domain that was matched.
- `people`: one entry per email address, with `name`, `title`, `company`, `campaigns` (refs), `replied`, `interested` (`null` when they carry no label), `opens`, `clicks` and `replies` added up over their records, `lastRepliedAt`, and `records` (one per campaign). Sorted by most recent reply.
- `totals`: `people`, `records`, `campaigns`, `replied`, `interested`, `leadsRead` and `leadsWithoutEmail`.
- `lastReply`: the most recent reply with a known time, or `null`.

- **Needs:** `leads.list`. Without it, `leads.byCampaign`, and the insight reads campaign by campaign. With neither it is `Unsupported`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of leads read. `max_leads` sets the ceiling.
- **Notes it can add:** the tool reports no per-lead open, click or reply counts, so those are `null`; a cap was reached. On a combined instance the note names only the tools whose records carry no counts, and is left out when every tool's records carry them: records from a tool that reports counts keep them.
- **Does not:** find a lead that has only a LinkedIn profile (they are counted in `leadsWithoutEmail`), or treat a subdomain as the same company. It reads leads and filters them here, so it costs the same whether the company has one lead or fifty.

## `leadCollisions`

People who are in two or more campaigns at once, so they get overlapping outreach.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `statuses` | `["active"]` | at least one of `active`, `paused`, `draft`, `completed`, `archived`, `unknown` | Campaign statuses to consider. |
| `max_campaigns` | 20 | 1 to 200 | The most campaigns to read. |
| `max_leads_per_campaign` | 500 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to read from each campaign. |

`data` (`LeadCollisions`):

- `collisions`: one entry per person, with `identifiers`, `campaignCount`, `stillActive` (campaigns in which the lead's own status is still `active`), `scope` (`across_tools` or `within_tool`), `providers`, `channels`, `campaigns` and a `finding` sentence. Most campaigns first.
- `totals`: `collisions`, `acrossTools`, `withinTool`, `campaignsRead`, `leadsRead` and `leadsWithoutIdentifier`.

- **Needs:** `leads.byCampaign`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of campaigns, plus one per page of each campaign's leads.
- **Notes it can add:** a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** count the same person twice in one campaign as a collision, or look at campaigns outside `statuses`. A lead with neither an email address nor a profile cannot be matched and is counted in `leadsWithoutIdentifier`.

## `suppressionCheck`

Leads sitting in campaigns although they are on the block list.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `statuses` | `["active"]` | at least one campaign status | Campaign statuses to check. |
| `max_block_entries` | 2,000 | 1 to 100,000 | The most block list entries to read. |
| `max_campaigns` | 20 | 1 to 200 | The most campaigns to check. |
| `max_leads_per_campaign` | 500 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to read from each campaign. |

`data` (`SuppressionCheck`):

- `violations`: one entry per lead, with `lead`, `email`, `linkedinUrl`, `leadStatus`, `campaign`, `campaignName`, `entry`, `entryValue`, `matchedBy` (`email`, `domain` or `linkedin`), `sameTool` and a `finding` sentence. Leads still `active` first.
- `byCampaign`: every campaign checked with `leadsRead` and `violations`, most violations first.
- `totals`: `violations`, `byMatch`, `campaignsRead`, `leadsRead`, `blockEntriesRead` and `blockEntriesNotChecked`.

- **Needs:** `blocklist`, `leads.byCampaign`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of block entries, plus one per page of each campaign's leads.
- **Notes it can add:** a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** match a company name or an entry of the vendor's kind `other`. Those are counted in `blockEntriesNotChecked`. It reports a lead once, by its most specific match: the address, then the profile, then the domain. It removes nobody from a campaign.

## `droppedReplies`

Replies from prospects that nobody answered, longest waiting first.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `since` | `null` | an ISO 8601 date, or date and time with offset | Only replies received at or after this. `null` means 14 days before now. |
| `older_than_hours` | 24 | 0 or more | A reply counts as dropped only after waiting this long without an answer. |
| `categories` | `["positive", "neutral", "unknown"]` | at least one reply category | Reply categories that need an answer. |
| `include_auto` | `false` | boolean | Also check replies the vendor marks as automatic. |
| `max_replies` | 50 | 1 to 1,000 | The most replies to read. |

`data` (`DroppedReplies`):

- `dropped`: one entry per conversation, with `reply` (the oldest unanswered reply), `lead` and the person's `name`, `email` and `linkedinUrl`, `campaign` and its `campaignName`, `channel`, `provider`, `category`, `subject`, `receivedAt`, `hoursWaiting`, `unansweredReplies`, `checkedBy` (`thread` or `timeline`) and a `finding` sentence.
- `totals`: `repliesRead`, `skippedByCategory`, `skippedAuto`, `conversations`, `answered`, `dropped`, `tooRecent` and `unchecked`.
- `since` and `olderThanHours`: what was used.

- **Needs:** `replies.list`. To check a conversation it also needs `messages.list` with `messages.byThread` or `leads.timeline`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of replies, one per conversation checked, and one read of each listed lead and campaign to name them. About `max_replies` requests plus two per listed reply.
- **Notes it can add:** the adapter cannot filter replies by date, so the newest were read and filtered here; the adapter cannot read one lead, so the names are `null`; a lead or campaign could not be read, so its names are `null`; the adapter can read neither a conversation nor a timeline, so no reply could be checked; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** report a reply it could not check as dropped. Those are counted in `totals.unchecked`. "Answered" means a message was sent after the reply in the same conversation. It does not read what the message said.

## `hotLeads`

Who to call first: leads ranked by a score built from what the vendor reports.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `campaign` | `null` | a campaign ref | Rank one campaign's leads. `null` ranks every lead the adapter lists. |
| `limit` | 20 | 1 to 500 | How many leads to return. |
| `max_leads` | 500 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to read and score. |
| `positive_labels` | `Interested`, `Meeting Booked`, `Meeting Completed` | at least 1 character each | Interest labels that count as positive. |
| `weight_interest` | 50 | 0 or more | Points for carrying a positive interest label. |
| `weight_reply` | 10 | 0 or more | Points for each reply, up to `count_cap`. |
| `weight_click` | 5 | 0 or more | Points for each click, up to `count_cap`. |
| `weight_open` | 1 | 0 or more | Points for each open, up to `count_cap`. |
| `weight_recency` | 20 | 0 or more | Points for a reply received right now. |
| `recency_days` | 14 | 1 or more | The recency points fade evenly to none over this many days. |
| `count_cap` | 5 | 1 or more, whole | The most replies, clicks or opens that earn points, so 40 opens do not outrank a reply. |

`data` (`HotLeads`):

- `leads`: highest score first, only leads that scored above 0. Each has `lead`, `email`, `linkedinUrl`, `name`, `title`, `company`, `campaign`, `status`, `interest`, `score`, `parts` (the points per signal: `interest`, `replies`, `clicks`, `opens`, `recency`), the raw `replies`, `clicks` and `opens`, `lastRepliedAt` and a `reason` sentence.
- `totals`: `leadsRead`, `scored`, `hot`, `withoutData` and `excluded`.

- **Needs:** `leads.list`, or `leads.byCampaign` when `campaign` is given. Without `campaign` on an adapter that cannot list every lead it is `Unsupported`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of leads read. `max_leads` sets the ceiling.
- **Notes it can add:** the tool reports no per-lead open, click or reply counts, so scores rest on the interest label and the lead's status; a cap was reached. On a combined instance the note names only the tools whose leads carry no counts.
- **Does not:** predict anything. The score is a sum you can recompute from `parts`. A part that is `null` means the vendor does not report that signal. A lead with no label and no engagement data is left out and counted in `withoutData`, never scored 0. Unsubscribed and bounced leads are left out and counted in `excluded`.

## `personalizationGaps`

Leads in one campaign who will get broken copy because a merge variable has no value for them.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `campaign` | required | a campaign ref | The campaign to check. |
| `limit` | 100 | 1 to 5,000 | How many affected leads to list. The counts cover all of them. |
| `max_leads` | 500 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to check. |

`data` (`PersonalizationGaps`):

- `campaign`: the ref that was checked.
- `variables`: one entry per variable in the copy, with `name`, `source` (`builtin`, `custom` or `unknown`), `gap`, `steps`, `uses` (step, variant, field, and whether that use has a fallback), `leadsWithoutValue` and `leadsAffected`. Most leads affected first.
- `leads`: the leads missing at least one variable, with `lead`, `email`, `linkedinUrl`, `status` and `missing`. Most missing first, up to `limit`.
- `unknownVariables`: variables no lead has a value for and that are not built in.
- `totals`: `steps`, `variables`, `leadsRead`, `leadsAffected` and `affectedRate`.

- **Needs:** `sequence`, `leads.byCampaign`.
- **Costs:** one request for the sequence, plus one per page of leads.
- **Notes it can add:** the sequence uses no variables, so no lead was read; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** render the copy. It finds `{{variable}}`, `{variable}` and `%variable%` in subjects and bodies, and checks each lead for a value: first name, last name, company, title, email, or a custom field of the same name without regard to case, spaces, underscores or hyphens. A variable with a fallback in every use, such as `{{firstName|there}}`, is not a gap. A variable no lead has, such as a sender signature the tool fills in, is listed as `unknown` and not counted against leads: check those by hand.
