Seven insights about the people in your campaigns. Each is a method on `outbound.insights` once the [insights plugin](/docs/insights) is added. What every answer carries (`data`, `meta.complete`, `meta.notes`) and how inputs are checked is on the [overview](/docs/insights).

People are matched by exact identifier only: an email address, lowercased, or a LinkedIn profile. Nothing is matched by name.

## The data the samples run on

Every sample on this page runs on the in-memory adapter with this data: one client on an email tool with three campaigns and on a LinkedIn tool with one. It runs at a fixed clock, 2026-11-14 12:00 UTC. See [how the samples are run](/docs/samples).

## `personSummary`

Everything about one person across campaigns and channels: each campaign they are in, how many messages they were sent, whether and how often they replied, which steps they replied to, and their interest label.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `person` | required | at least 3 characters | An email address, a LinkedIn profile URL, or a lead ref as the other people insights return it. Anything else is `Invalid`. |
| `max_campaigns` | 20 | 1 to 50 | The most campaigns to report on. |

`data` (`PersonSummary`):

- `found`: `false` when no record matched.
- `identifiers`: every exact identifier the records carry, as `email:` and `linkedin:` keys.
- `campaigns`: one entry per campaign, with `campaignName`, `campaignStatus`, `channel`, `provider`, `lead`, `leadStatus`, `vendorLeadStatus`, `interest` (the vendor's own label), `messagesSent`, `replies`, `replied`, `autoReplied`, `repliedStepIndexes` (counted from 0) and `repliedStepNumbers` (counted from 1), `lastStepIndex` and `lastStepNumber`, `firstContactedAt`, `lastContactedAt` and `lastRepliedAt`.
- `totals`: `campaigns`, `campaignsReplied`, `campaignReplyRate` (campaigns they replied in, divided by campaigns that sent them at least one message), `messagesSent`, `replies` and `byChannel`.

A record the vendor labels out-of-office or bounced (the same rule as [`hotLeads`](#hotleads)) is `autoReplied: true`: its replies were automatic, so it counts 0 `replies`, is not `replied` and adds nothing to `campaignsReplied`. A reply in the thread flagged automatic (`isAuto: true`, by the vendor or a fixed rule) is not counted either, and a record whose every reply is flagged automatic is `autoReplied: true` too. When the vendor's status is `replied` only because of those automatic replies, `leadStatus` is where the lead is in the sequence instead (for example `completed`), so it agrees with `replied`; `vendorLeadStatus` keeps the vendor's status as it came.

- **Needs:** `leads.lookup`, `leads.timeline`, and `leads.get` for a lead ref.
- **Costs:** one lookup per identifier, one read of the lead for a lead ref, then up to 2 requests per campaign (the lead's timeline and the campaign).
- **Notes it can add:** a thread held fewer sent messages than the lead's last step, so messages sent is counted from the last step; a record labelled out-of-office or bounced had replies that are not counted; a thread held replies flagged automatic that are not counted; redact masks one kind of identifier, so records found only under it were not linked.
- **Does not:** match by name, or find a LinkedIn record from an email address unless some tool holds both for that person. `repliedSteps` is empty when the vendor ties a reply to no step.
- **Refuses:** with `Unsupported`, before any read, while the [`redact` plugin](/docs/plugins/redact) masks the kind of identifier asked for, or masks any identifier when given a lead ref. Masked records could not be matched, so it does not report a known person as not found. While redact masks both `email` and `linkedin`, as `outbound mcp` does by default, it is not offered as a tool.

## `accountSummary`

Everyone at one company, across campaigns and tools, by exact email domain.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `domain` | required | at least 3 characters | The company's domain, an email address at it, or a URL on it. |
| `allow_free_domains` | `false` | boolean | Accept an email provider domain such as `gmail.com`, which is not one company. |
| `max_leads` | 1,000 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to read in all: at the domain when the adapter searches by domain, in the workspace when it scans. |
| `max_campaigns` | 20 | 1 to 200 | The most campaigns to read leads from, when the adapter cannot list every lead at once. |
| `positive_labels` | `Interested`, `Meeting Booked`, `Meeting Completed` | at least 1 character each | Vendor interest labels that count as interested, matched without regard to case. |

`data` (`AccountSummary`):

- `domain`: the domain that was matched.
- `foundBy`: `search` when the adapter found the domain's leads itself (`leads.byDomain`), `scan` when leads were read in order up to `max_leads` and matched here.
- `finding`: one sentence on who was found. After a scan that stopped with leads unread and found nobody, it says so: `Checked the first 1,000 leads and more exist; none matched. This does not mean nobody at acme.com is in a campaign.`
- `people`: one entry per email address, with `name`, `title`, `company`, `campaigns` (refs), `replied`, `interested` (`null` when they carry no label), `opens`, `clicks` and `replies` added up over their records, `lastRepliedAt`, and `records` (one per campaign, each with `autoReplied`). Sorted by most recent reply. A record the vendor labels out-of-office or bounced is `autoReplied: true`, and its replies do not count toward `replied`, `replies` or `lastRepliedAt`.
- `totals`: `people`, `records`, `campaigns`, `replied`, `interested`, `leadsRead`, `leadsWithoutEmail` and `moreLeadsExist` (`true` when `max_leads` or `max_campaigns` left leads unread, so people may be missing).
- `lastReply`: the most recent reply with a known time, or `null`.

- **Needs:** `leads.byDomain`, and the adapter finds the domain's leads wherever they are in the workspace. Without it, `leads.list`, and the insight reads leads in order up to `max_leads`; without that, `leads.byCampaign`, and it reads campaign by campaign. With none of them it is `Unsupported`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of leads read. `max_leads` sets the ceiling. With `leads.byDomain` only the domain's leads are read, usually one or two requests. Once a lead has replies, one or two more for up to 200 of the newest replies, the campaign's when `campaign` is given, to find leads whose every reply was automatic.
- **Notes it can add:** the tool reports no per-lead open, click or reply counts, so those are `null`; a cap was reached. On a combined instance the note names only the tools whose records carry no counts, and is left out when every tool's records carry them: records from a tool that reports counts keep them.
- **Does not:** find a lead that has only a LinkedIn profile (a scan counts them in `leadsWithoutEmail`; a search by domain never reads them), or treat a subdomain as the same company. Without `leads.byDomain` it reads leads and filters them here, so it costs the same whether the company has one lead or fifty, and in a workspace larger than `max_leads` it can miss the company entirely: `totals.moreLeadsExist` and `finding` then say so.
- **Refuses:** with `Unsupported`, before any read, while the [`redact` plugin](/docs/plugins/redact) masks `email`, which leaves no domain to match. It is then not offered as a tool.

## `leadCollisions`

People who are in two or more campaigns at once, so they get overlapping outreach. By default a person counts only when their own status is `active` in two or more of the campaigns read.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `statuses` | `["active"]` | at least one of `active`, `paused`, `draft`, `completed`, `archived`, `unknown` | Campaign statuses to consider. |
| `include_past` | `false` | `true` or `false` | Also report people in two or more campaigns who are active in fewer than two, such as one who completed a campaign and is now in another. |
| `max_campaigns` | 20 | 1 to 200 | The most campaigns to read. |
| `max_leads_per_campaign` | 500 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to read from each campaign. |

`data` (`LeadCollisions`):

- `collisions`: one entry per person, with `identifiers`, `campaignCount`, `stillActive` (campaigns in which the lead's own status is still `active`), `scope` (`across_tools` or `within_tool`), `providers`, `channels`, `campaigns` and a `finding` sentence. The sentence says "at once" only when `stillActive` is 2 or more; otherwise it names each stopped campaign with the lead's status there. Most active campaigns first, then most campaigns.
- `totals`: `collisions`, `concurrent` (collisions returned with `stillActive` 2 or more), `pastOverlaps` (people in two or more campaigns but active in fewer than two, returned only with `include_past`), `acrossTools`, `withinTool`, `campaignsRead`, `leadsRead` and `leadsWithoutIdentifier`.

- **Needs:** `leads.byCampaign`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of campaigns, plus one per page of each campaign's leads.
- **Notes it can add:** a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** count the same person twice in one campaign as a collision, or look at campaigns outside `statuses`. A lead with neither an email address nor a profile cannot be matched and is counted in `leadsWithoutIdentifier`.
- **Refuses:** with `Unsupported`, before any read, while the [`redact` plugin](/docs/plugins/redact) masks `email` with its default `mask` strategy, since every address would read the same. It is then not offered as a tool. Under `strategy: "hash"` it matches by the hashed address, and notes that LinkedIn profiles could not be matched when those are masked too.

## `suppressionCheck`

Leads sitting in campaigns although they are on the block list.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `statuses` | `["active"]` | at least one campaign status | Campaign statuses to check. |
| `lead_statuses` | `["active", "paused", "unknown"]` | at least one of `active`, `paused`, `unknown`, `completed`, `skipped`, `replied`, `bounced`, `unsubscribed` | Lead statuses that count as a violation: by default the leads still being sent to, or yet to start. |
| `max_block_entries` | 2,000 | 1 to 100,000 | The most block list entries to read. |
| `max_campaigns` | 20 | 1 to 200 | The most campaigns to check. |
| `max_leads_per_campaign` | 500 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to read from each campaign. |

`data` (`SuppressionCheck`):

- `violations`: one entry per lead whose status is in `lead_statuses`, with `lead`, `email`, `linkedinUrl`, `leadStatus`, `campaign`, `campaignName`, `entry`, `entryValue`, `matchedBy` (`email`, `domain` or `linkedin`), `sameTool` and a `finding` sentence. Leads still `active` first.
- `byCampaign`: every campaign checked with `leadsRead` and `violations`, most violations first.
- `totals`: `violations`, `alreadyStopped` (leads on the block list whose status is not in `lead_statuses`, most often because they unsubscribed, which is how many tools put them on the list), `byMatch` (of the violations), `campaignsRead`, `leadsRead`, `blockEntriesRead`, `blockEntriesNotChecked`, `moreBlockEntriesExist` (`true` when `max_block_entries` left entries unread; a lead on one of those was not found, and `blockEntriesNotChecked` is then `null`) and `moreLeadsExist` (`true` when `max_leads_per_campaign` left a campaign's leads unread).

- **Needs:** `blocklist`, `leads.byCampaign`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of block entries, plus one per page of each campaign's leads.
- **Notes it can add:** a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** match a company name or an entry of the vendor's kind `other`. Those are counted in `blockEntriesNotChecked`. It reports a lead once, by its most specific match: the address, then the profile, then the domain. It removes nobody from a campaign.
- **Refuses:** with `Unsupported`, before any read, while the [`redact` plugin](/docs/plugins/redact) masks `email` with its default `mask` strategy. It is then not offered as a tool. Under `strategy: "hash"` it still matches an exact address, and says the domain entries (and, when profiles are masked, the profile entries) could not be matched.

## `droppedReplies`

Replies from prospects that nobody answered, longest waiting first.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `since` | `null` | an ISO 8601 date, or date and time with offset | Only replies received at or after this. `null` means 14 days before now. |
| `older_than_hours` | 24 | 0 or more | A reply counts as dropped only after waiting this long without an answer. |
| `categories` | `["positive", "neutral", "unknown"]` | at least one reply category | Reply categories that need an answer. |
| `include_auto` | `false` | boolean | Also check replies the vendor marks as automatic. |
| `max_replies` | 50 | 1 to 1,000 | The most replies to read. |

`data` (`DroppedReplies`):

- `dropped`: one entry per conversation, with `reply` (the oldest unanswered reply), `lead` and the person's `name`, `email` and `linkedinUrl`, `campaign` and its `campaignName`, `channel`, `provider`, `category`, `subject`, `receivedAt`, `hoursWaiting`, `unansweredReplies`, `checkedBy` (`thread` or `timeline`) and a `finding` sentence.
- `totals`: `repliesRead`, `skippedByCategory`, `skippedAuto`, `conversations`, `answered`, `dropped`, `tooRecent`, `unchecked` and `moreRepliesExist` (`true` when `max_replies` left replies unread, so dropped ones may be missing).
- `since` and `olderThanHours`: what was used.

- **Needs:** `replies.list`. To check a conversation it also needs `messages.list` with `messages.byThread` or `leads.timeline`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of replies, one per conversation checked, and one read of each listed lead and campaign to name them. About `max_replies` requests plus two per listed reply.
- **Notes it can add:** the adapter cannot filter replies by date, so the newest were read and filtered here; the adapter cannot read one lead, so the names are `null`; a reply carries no lead ref (Smartlead ties a reply to no lead), so its names are `null`; a lead or campaign could not be read, so its names are `null`; the adapter can read neither a conversation nor a timeline, so no reply could be checked; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** report a reply it could not check as dropped. Those are counted in `totals.unchecked`. "Answered" means a message was sent after the reply in the same conversation. It does not read what the message said.

## `hotLeads`

Who to call first: leads ranked by a score built from what the vendor reports.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `campaign` | `null` | a campaign ref | Rank one campaign's leads. An unknown campaign is `NotFound`. `null` ranks every lead the adapter lists. |
| `limit` | 20 | 1 to 500 | How many leads to return. |
| `max_leads` | 500 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads a lead listing reads: the leads labelled positive where the adapter filters by interest, and the lead list where it cannot or `include_engagement_only` is set. |
| `max_replies` | 1,000 | 1 to 20,000 | The most replies within `recency_days` to read. A safety ceiling, not a sample. |
| `max_age_days` | 90 | 1 or more | A positive label keeps its points while the last reply is within `recency_days` and fades evenly to none at this many days. A lead whose last reply is older ranks after every fresher one. |
| `include_engagement_only` | `false` | `true` or `false` | Also walk the lead list up to `max_leads`, for leads whose only signal is opens or clicks. |
| `positive_labels` | `Interested`, `Meeting Booked`, `Meeting Completed` | at least 1 character each | Interest labels that count as positive. |
| `weight_interest` | 50 | 0 or more | Points for carrying a positive interest label. |
| `weight_reply` | 10 | 0 or more | Points for each reply, up to `count_cap`. |
| `weight_click` | 5 | 0 or more | Points for each click, up to `count_cap`. |
| `weight_open` | 1 | 0 or more | Points for each open, up to `count_cap`. |
| `weight_recency` | 20 | 0 or more | Points for a reply received right now. |
| `recency_days` | 14 | 1 or more | The recency points fade evenly to none over this many days. |
| `count_cap` | 5 | 1 or more, whole | The most replies, clicks or opens that earn points, so 40 opens do not outrank a reply. |

`data` (`HotLeads`):

- `leads`: highest score first: the leads that scored above 0, then, while `limit` leaves room, the leads labelled negative, which score 0. Each has `lead`, `email`, `linkedinUrl`, `name`, `title`, `company`, `campaign`, `status`, `interest`, `score`, `parts` (the points per signal: `interest`, `replies`, `clicks`, `opens`, `recency`), the raw `replies`, `clicks` and `opens`, `lastRepliedAt` and a `reason` sentence.
- `totals`: `leadsRead`, `scored`, `hot`, `withoutData`, `excluded`, `moreLeadsExist` (`true` when `max_leads` stopped a lead listing with leads left unread), `repliesRead` (replies within `recency_days`), `fromReplies` (leads no listing read, read because they replied), `unreadable` (leads that replied and could not be read, probably deleted), `stale` (leads with a score whose last reply is older than `max_age_days`), `autoReplied` (leads labelled out-of-office or bounced, or whose every reply read was flagged automatic, whose replies earned nothing) and `negative` (leads labelled negative, which earned no points at all).

Hot leads are the people who replied or were labelled interested, a small set bounded by time, so `hotLeads` starts from that signal instead of walking the lead list in the vendor's order. It reads every reply within `recency_days`, and, where the adapter filters by interest (`leads.byInterest`), the leads it labels positive. It then reads the leads behind those replies that no listing returned: many addresses in one request where the adapter can (`leads.lookupMany`), otherwise one request each for the newest `limit`. A reply in the window also fills in a lead's reply count and last reply time when its record reports less, so a vendor whose leads carry no counts still gets recency. Where the adapter cannot filter by interest, or `include_engagement_only` is set, the lead list is walked as well, up to `max_leads`.

An interest label fades so an old label does not outrank a fresh reply: it keeps its points while the last reply is within `recency_days`, fades to none at `max_age_days`, and a lead whose last reply is older than `max_age_days` is listed after every fresher lead.

While the [redact plugin](/docs/plugins/redact) masks email addresses, which an Instantly reply names its lead by, the leads found only through a reply are not looked up, and under the default `mask` strategy replies are not read at all. Each says so in a note and the answer is incomplete; the leads labelled positive are still found by the vendor's filter.

Some labels discount a lead. An automatic one discounts its replies: it earns no reply or recency points and no "replied" reason, and its `reason` names the label, "marked Out of office, so replies earn nothing". Its clicks and opens still count. A negative one discounts everything: the lead earns no points at all, from clicks and opens either, so it never outranks a lead that engaged; it is listed after every lead with a score above 0 while `limit` leaves room, and its `reason` reads "marked Not Interested, so it earns no points".

| Discounted as | `interestCategory` | Or, where the vendor gives no category, a label that reads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automatic | `ooo`, `bounce` | "out of office" |
| Negative | `negative`, `unsubscribe` | "not interested", "wrong person", "unsubscribe(d)" or "do not contact" |

A lead is also discounted as automatic when every one of its replies read is flagged automatic (`isAuto: true`, by the vendor or a fixed rule); its `reason` reads "every reply read was automatic, so replies earn nothing", and `totals.autoReplied` counts both kinds. An automatic reply is discounted because a vendor's per-lead reply count may include it; [Coverage](/docs/coverage) says, per vendor, what is known about that count. A negative one is discounted because the person asked not to hear more. A label listed in `positive_labels` is never discounted as negative. [`personSummary`](#personsummary) and [`accountSummary`](#accountsummary) use the same automatic rule to decide whether a person replied.

- **Needs:** `leads.byInterest`, or else `leads.list` (`leads.byCampaign` when `campaign` is given). Without `campaign` on an adapter that can do neither it is `Unsupported`. Reading replies needs `replies.list`; without it a note says the leads were ranked from their records alone.
- **Costs:** one request per page of replies in the window, one per page of leads labelled positive (on Instantly at least five, one per positive status), one per 100 addresses looked up, and one per other replied lead read, at most `limit`. On Instantly with about 180 replies in 14 days that is about 10.
- **Notes it can add:** the tool reports no per-lead open, click or reply counts, so clicks and opens are unknown and reply counts and recency come from the replies read; redact kept replies from being matched; a cap was reached. On a combined instance the note names only the tools whose leads carry no counts.
- **Does not:** predict anything. The score is a sum you can recompute from `parts`. A part that is `null` means the vendor does not report that signal. A lead with no label and no engagement data is left out and counted in `withoutData`, never scored 0. Unsubscribed and bounced leads are left out and counted in `excluded`.

## `personalizationGaps`

Leads in one campaign who will get broken copy because a merge variable has no value for them.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `campaign` | required | a campaign ref | The campaign to check. |
| `limit` | 100 | 1 to 5,000 | How many affected leads to list. The counts cover all of them. |
| `max_leads` | 500 | 1 to 20,000 | The most leads to check. |

`data` (`PersonalizationGaps`):

- `campaign`: the ref that was checked, even when the input was its bare vendor id.
- `variables`: one entry per variable in the copy, with `name`, `source` (`builtin`, `custom` or `unknown`), `sender` (`true` for a variable the tool fills from the sending account; its `source` is `unknown`), `gap` (`true` when at least one lead read gets broken copy from it), `steps`, `uses` (step, variant, field, and whether that use has a fallback), `leadsWithoutValue` and `leadsAffected`. Most leads affected first.
- `leads`: the leads missing at least one variable, with `lead`, `email`, `linkedinUrl`, `status` and `missing`. Most missing first, up to `limit`.
- `unknownVariables`: variables no lead has a value for and that are neither built in nor filled from the sender.
- `totals`: `steps`, `variables`, `leadsRead`, `leadsAffected` and `affectedRate`.

- **Needs:** `sequence`, `leads.byCampaign`.
- **Costs:** one request for the sequence, plus one per page of leads.
- **Notes it can add:** the sequence uses no variables, so no lead was read; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** render the copy. It finds `{{variable}}`, `{variable}` and `%variable%` in subjects and bodies, and checks each lead for a value: first name, last name, company, title, email, or a custom field of the same name without regard to case, spaces, underscores or hyphens. A variable with a fallback in every use, such as `{{firstName|there}}`, is not a gap. A variable the tool fills from the sending account, such as Instantly's `{{accountSignature}}`, `{{sendingAccountName}}`, `{{sendingAccountFirstName}}` and `{{sendingAccountEmail}}`, has `sender: true` and is left out of `unknownVariables`. Any other variable no lead has is listed in `unknownVariables`: check those by hand. Neither is counted against leads.
