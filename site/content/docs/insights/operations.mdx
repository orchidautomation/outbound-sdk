Eight insights computed from campaign stats. Each is a method on `outbound.insights` once the [insights plugin](/docs/insights) is added. What every answer carries and how inputs are checked is on the [overview](/docs/insights).

Three things hold for all of them:

- **A reply rate is replies divided by leads contacted**, or by messages sent when the vendor does not count leads. A bounce rate is bounces divided by messages sent.
- **A rate over a time window is per message sent.** `periodComparison` and `clientReport` divide replies and positive replies by messages sent in the period, and say so in `replyRateBasis: "sent"`. A week's replies also answer leads first contacted weeks before, so a week that contacted 5 new leads and sent 300 follow-ups could otherwise show 8 replies as 160%. Where a period does not report messages sent, as some vendors do not, both periods are divided by leads contacted instead, with `replyRateBasis: "contacted"` and a sentence that reads "Replies per lead contacted went from …". The two periods are never on different bases.
- **A rate comes with its count, its base and its 95% interval** as `count`, `of`, `rate`, `low` and `high`.
- **"The last N days" means N complete UTC days ending yesterday.** Today is left out because it is not over.
- **Every campaign row has `campaign`, its ref, and `campaignName`.**

Five of them (`periodComparison`, `campaignRanking`, `channelMix`, `tagRollup` and `clientReport`) share two inputs:

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `statuses` | `["active"]` | at least one campaign status | Only campaigns in one of these states are read. Up to 0.7 the default also read paused and completed campaigns; pass `["active", "paused", "completed"]` to keep that. |
| `max_campaigns` | 50 | 1 to 200 | The most campaigns to read. |

Their `data` says how many campaigns were read in `campaignsRead`, and `moreCampaignsExist` is `true` when `max_campaigns` stopped the listing with campaigns left. Those campaigns are in no part of the answer: a campaign past the cap can be the best one, or the one with the bounce problem.

`campaignRanking`, `channelMix`, `tagRollup` and `campaignDiagnosis` also take a date range:

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `from` | `null` | `YYYY-MM-DD` | First day counted. `null` for the whole history. |
| `to` | `null` | `YYYY-MM-DD` | Last day counted. `null` for up to today. |

## `campaignDiagnosis`

Why one campaign is underperforming: a list of findings, most serious first.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `campaign` | required | a campaign ref | The campaign to diagnose. |
| `from`, `to` | `null` | `YYYY-MM-DD` or `null` | The days the totals cover. `null` for the whole history, the default. |
| `min_sent` | 50 | 0 or more, whole | Under this many sends, rates are not judged. |
| `min_reply_rate` | 0.01 | 0 to 1 | A reply rate under this is a finding. |
| `max_bounce_rate` | 0.03 | 0 to 1 | A bounce rate over this is a finding. |
| `min_positive_share` | 0.2 | 0 to 1 | A share of replies that are positive under this is a finding. |
| `days` | 7 | 1 to 30 | The days, ending yesterday, held against the same number before. |
| `max_send_drop` | 0.3 | 0 to 1 | A fall in sends, recent days against the days before, over this share is a finding. |
| `min_weekly_hours` | 20 | 0 to 168 | Sending windows open fewer hours a week than this limit volume. |
| `daily_limit_share` | 0.9 | 0 to 1 | Average daily sends at or over this share of the daily limit mean the limit holds volume back. |
| `max_senders` | 500 | 1 to 500 | The most of the campaign's own senders to read and screen. |
| `max_leads` | 2,000 | 1 to 50,000 | The most leads to read when sends fell or stopped, to tell lead runout from a fault. |

`data` (`CampaignDiagnosis`):

- `campaign`, `campaignName`, `status`, `channel`, `provider`.
- `period`: the `from` and `to` given.
- `totals`: `sent`, `contacted`, `replied`, `positive`, `bounced`.
- `rates`: `replyRate`, `positiveRate` and `bounceRate`, each with its interval.
- `findings`: each with a `code`, a `severity`, a `finding` sentence, the `numbers` behind it and `next`, what to look at. Empty when nothing was found in what could be checked.
- `notChecked`: what could not be checked, and why.
- `summary`: the count of findings and the most serious one.

The findings:

| Code | Severity | Fires when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `high_bounce_rate` | high | The bounce rate is over `max_bounce_rate`. |
| `contacted_exceeds_leads` | high | Leads contacted are more than twice the campaign's leads, so the contacted count is not unique leads and the per-lead rates are off. Needs `campaigns.progress`. A little over is normal: leads removed after starting stay counted. |
| `out_of_leads` | high | Sends fell or stopped and under a day of leads is left to start at the recent pace of new leads started, the pace `lead_runway` uses. It replaces `falling_sends` or `no_recent_sends`: the cause is lead runout, not a fault. |
| `no_recent_sends` | high | The campaign is active but nothing was sent in the last `days`. |
| `inactive_sender` | high | A sender of the campaign is not active or has a warmup score of 0. Every sender of the campaign is screened, up to `max_senders`. |
| `low_reply_rate` | high or medium | The reply rate is under `min_reply_rate`. High when the whole interval is under it. |
| `too_little_volume` | medium | Fewer sends than `min_sent`. Reply and bounce rates are then not judged. |
| `few_positive_replies` | medium | The positive share of replies is under `min_positive_share`. |
| `falling_sends` | medium | Sends fell by more than `max_send_drop`. |
| `short_sequence` | medium | The sequence has one or two steps. |
| `schedule_limits_volume` | low | The windows are open too few hours a week, or recent days run at the daily limit. |
| `tracking_off` | low | Open or click tracking is off, so a missing open or click count is expected. |

- **Needs:** `stats.campaign`. It checks more when the adapter has `campaigns.progress`, `campaigns.settings`, `sequence`, `senders.byCampaign` and `stats.byDay`.
- **Costs:** up to 7 requests, plus one per page of the campaign's senders and, only when sends fell or stopped, one per page of its leads (up to `max_leads`).
- **Notes it can add:** one per part it could not check, the same sentences as `notChecked`: schedule and tracking, sequence length, sender status, recent sending trend.
- **Does not:** say all is well. An empty list means nothing was found in what could be checked. Read `notChecked` beside it. It does not read the copy: use `copyAudit` and `stepFunnel` for that.

## `pipelineForecast`

The leads one campaign has left, and what they should produce. It is an estimate and says so.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `campaign` | required | a campaign ref | The campaign to forecast. |
| `days` | 7 | 1 to 60 | How many complete days, ending yesterday, set the daily pace. |
| `max_leads` | 2,000 | 1 to 50,000 | The most leads to read. |

`data` (`PipelineForecast`):

- `campaign`, `campaignName`, `status`, `channel`, `provider`.
- `estimate`: always `true`. Every figure below `remaining` is a projection, not a count.
- `leadsRead`, `leadsComplete` (`false` when `max_leads` stopped the read, so `remaining` is a minimum), `byStatus`.
- `remaining`: leads still `active` in the campaign. A `skipped` lead, one the vendor decided never to send to, is not remaining and is not of unknown status.
- `notStarted`, `inSequence`: the active leads split by whether their sequence has started, from the lead's last step sent or last contact time. Both are `null` when no lead read reports either, and then every active lead counts as not started.
- `dailyContactRate`: new leads started per day over the last `days`, from the daily `contacted` count. It is never emails sent: most sends are follow-ups to leads already in sequence. `null` when the vendor reports no daily contacted count.
- `daysLeft`: `notStarted` (or `remaining` when that is `null`) divided by `dailyContactRate`, and `0` when every lead was read and none is left to start, whatever the pace; the `finding` then says to add leads. `daysLeft` is `null` only when the leads left are unknown (`max_leads` stopped the read, or a lead's status is `unknown`), or when some are left and there is no pace to divide by. The `lead_runway` tool returns the same figure as `runway_days`.
- `replyRate`, `positiveRate`: the campaign's rates so far, over its whole history.
- `expectedReplies`, `expectedPositive`: `expected`, `low` and `high`, from the rate and the ends of its interval.
- `assumptions`: what the estimate assumes, as sentences.
- `finding`: the answer in a sentence.

- **Needs:** `leads.byCampaign`, `stats.campaign`. Days left also needs `stats.byDay`.
- **Costs:** one request per page of leads, plus two stats requests.
- **Notes it can add:** days of sending left were not estimated because the adapter has no stats by day; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** know the future. It assumes every remaining lead is contacted, that they reply at the rate the campaign has had so far, and that the daily pace of new leads started stays what it was. Leads partway through the sequence may reply less. A day with no report counts as none started.

## `anomalies`

Days that broke from trend, for one campaign or the whole workspace.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `campaign` | `null` | a campaign ref | `null` checks the whole workspace. |
| `days` | 30 | 1 to 90 | How many complete days, ending yesterday, are checked. |
| `baseline_days` | 14 | 5 to 60 | How many days before a day form the baseline it is held to. |
| `metrics` | `["sent", "replied", "bounced"]` | at least one of `sent`, `contacted`, `replied`, `positive`, `bounced`, `opened`, `clicked`, `unsubscribed` | The daily counts to check. |
| `threshold` | 2.5 | above 0 | How many standard deviations from the baseline mean is unusual. |
| `min_baseline_points` | 5 | 2 or more, whole | The fewest baseline days with data a day needs to be tested. |

`data` (`Anomalies`):

- `scope`: the campaign's ref, or `workspace`.
- `period`: the days checked. `threshold`: what was used.
- `anomalies`: newest first, each with `date`, `metric`, `value`, `baselineMean`, `baselinePoints`, `zScore`, `judgedBy`, `direction` (`above` or `below`) and a `finding` sentence. `judgedBy` is `z_score` for a day `threshold` standard deviations or more from the baseline mean. It is `flat_baseline` for a day that differs from a baseline whose days all had the same count: there is no standard deviation then, so `zScore` is `null`.
- `metrics`: for each metric, `daysWithData`, `daysTested`, `testable`, and the `reason` when no day could be tested.

- **Needs:** `stats.byDay`, and `stats.workspace` with `campaign: null` or `stats.campaign` with a campaign. On an adapter without `stats.workspace` the workspace call is refused as `Unsupported` before any request, with "not available for this input" in the message: pass a campaign there.
- **Costs:** one stats request.
- **Notes it can add:** none of its own.
- **Does not:** read a day the vendor has no count for as zero. Such days are skipped. A day is tested only when its baseline has at least `min_baseline_points` days. Against a flat baseline `threshold` does not apply: any other count is flagged, however small the difference. A break that lasts becomes part of its own baseline, so only its first days are flagged.

## `periodComparison`

The last `days` complete days against the `days` before, per campaign and in total.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `days` | 7 | 1 to 90 | The length of each period. |
| `alpha` | 0.05 | above 0, under 1 | The p-value under which a change counts as more than chance. |
| `min_sent` | 50 | 0 or more, whole | A campaign under this many sends in either period is listed apart. |
| `statuses`, `max_campaigns` | see above |  |  |

`data` (`PeriodComparison`):

- `days`, `current`, `previous`: the two periods as `from` and `to`.
- `campaignsRead`, `moreCampaignsExist`.
- `campaigns`: one entry per campaign, with `current` and `previous` (counts and the three rates; replies and positive replies per message sent, or per lead contacted when a period does not report sends), `replyRateBasis` (`"sent"` or `"contacted"`, the same for both periods), `change` (current minus previous; rates change in points, so 0.02 is two points), `replyRateTest` (`z`, `pValue`, `significant`), `verdict` (`up`, `down`, `no_real_change` or `unknown`) and a `finding` sentence ("Replies per message sent went from 6% to 12%; ..."). Largest change in reply rate first.
- `tooFewSends`: campaigns under `min_sent` in either period, with `currentSent` and `previousSent`.
- `total`: the same comparison for every campaign read, added together. Each rate counts only the campaigns that report both its count and what it is divided by, so the bounces of the campaigns that report them are never divided by every campaign's sends.
- `workspace`: the same for the vendor's own workspace totals, or `null` when the adapter has none. On a combined instance the tools' rows are added up by the same rule.

- **Needs:** `stats.campaign`.
- **Costs:** two stats requests per campaign, plus two for the workspace.
- **Notes it can add:** workspace totals were left out because the adapter has no workspace stats; a rate of `total` or `workspace` leaves out campaigns (or tools) that do not report one side of it, naming the rate and how many are counted; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** test anything but the reply rate. The other changes are plain differences. `verdict` is `unknown` when a period has no reply or send counts, or neither period has a reply. A campaign whose vendor reports no sends has no reply rate for the period; it is never divided by leads contacted instead.

## `campaignRanking`

Which campaigns are best, ranked so a small sample cannot win on luck.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `metric` | `reply_rate` | `reply_rate`, `positive_reply_rate`, `bounce_rate` | The rate to rank by. For `bounce_rate`, lower is better. |
| `min_sent` | 50 | 0 or more, whole | Campaigns with fewer sends than this are left out of the rank. |
| `from`, `to`, `statuses`, `max_campaigns` | see above |  |  |

`data` (`CampaignRanking`):

- `metric`, `better` (`higher` or `lower`), `period`, `campaignsRead`, `moreCampaignsExist`.
- `ranked`: best first, each with `rank`, `sent` (messages sent, `null` when not reported), and the rate as `count`, `of`, `rate`, `low` and `high`, with `basis` saying what `of` counts: `contacted` (leads contacted) or `sent`, which it is for a campaign that does not count leads and always for bounce rate.
- `tooFewSends`: campaigns under `min_sent`, with the `reason`.
- `unknown`: campaigns whose count for this metric the vendor does not report, with the `reason`.
- `gap`: `top` (the rate of the campaign ranked first), `median` and `difference`.

- **Needs:** `stats.campaign`.
- **Costs:** one listing per status, plus one stats request per campaign (one for all of them on a vendor that reports every campaign in one call with every count).
- **Notes it can add:** a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** rank by the raw rate. It ranks by the low end of the 95% interval, or for a bounce rate by the high end, lowest first. A campaign with a `null` rate is in `unknown`, never ranked last.

## `channelMix`

Email against LinkedIn, from campaign totals added up per channel.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `alpha` | 0.05 | above 0, under 1 | The p-value under which a difference counts as more than chance. |
| `from`, `to`, `statuses`, `max_campaigns` | see above |  |  |

`data` (`ChannelMix`):

- `period`, `campaignsRead`, `moreCampaignsExist`.
- `channels`: email first, then LinkedIn, only the channels that have campaigns. Each has `campaigns`, `sent`, `contacted`, `replied`, `positive`, `bounced`, `replyRate`, `positiveRate` and `acceptance` (connection requests accepted out of those sent; `null` for email).
- `comparison`: `higher` (the channel with the higher reply rate), `difference` and the `test`. `null` when there is one channel, or a channel has no counts to compare.
- `finding`: the answer in a sentence.
- `byProvider`: the same numbers for each tool and channel.

- **Needs:** `stats.campaign`.
- **Costs:** one listing per status, plus one stats request per campaign (one for all of them on a vendor that reports every campaign in one call with every count).
- **Notes it can add:** a channel's rate leaves out campaigns that do not report one side of it; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** make the two channels comparable. A rate counts only the campaigns that report both its count and what it is divided by, so its `count` and `of` can be smaller than the channel's totals beside it. A LinkedIn reply and an email reply are counted the same way, but the audiences usually differ. With one channel present it returns that channel and says there is nothing to compare.

## `tagRollup`

Results grouped by campaign tag, for example one tag per client, offer or audience.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `min_sent` | 50 | 0 or more, whole | Tags with fewer sends than this are listed apart. |
| `from`, `to`, `statuses`, `max_campaigns` | see above |  |  |

`data` (`TagRollup`):

- `period`, `campaigns` (campaigns read), `campaignsRead` (the same number) and `moreCampaignsExist`.
- `tags`: one entry per tag, with `tag` (`null` for campaigns that carry none), `campaigns`, `sent`, `contacted`, `replied`, `positive`, `bounced`, and `replyRate`, `positiveRate` and `bounceRate` with their intervals. Best first, by the low end of the reply rate interval.
- `tooFewSends`: tags under `min_sent`.

- **Needs:** `stats.campaign`, and campaigns that carry tags. On an adapter whose campaigns have no tags (HeyReach; `outbound.fields.campaign.tags` is `false`) it is `Unsupported` before any request and left out of the tool list, since every campaign would be untagged. On a combined instance one tool with tags is enough; the other tool's campaigns are under the `null` tag.
- **Costs:** one listing per status, plus one stats request per campaign (one for all of them on a vendor that reports every campaign in one call with every count).
- **Notes it can add:** a tag's rate leaves out campaigns that do not report one side of it, naming the rate and how many campaigns are counted; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** split a campaign between its tags. A campaign with two tags counts in full in both, so the tags do not add up to the workspace. A rate counts only the campaigns that report both its count and what it is divided by, so its `count` and `of` can be smaller than the tag's totals beside it.

## `clientReport`

One report for one client or workspace over the last `days` complete days, composed from what the other insights on this page compute.

| Input | Default | Range | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `days` | 7 | 1 to 90 | The length of the period. |
| `limit` | 3 | 1 to 20 | How many best and worst campaigns to show in `top` and `bottom`. |
| `alpha` | 0.05 | above 0, under 1 | The p-value under which a change counts as more than chance. |
| `min_sent` | 50 | 0 or more, whole | Campaigns under this many sends in the period are not judged. |
| `max_bounce_rate` | 0.03 | 0 to 1 | A campaign bounce rate over this is a bounce problem. |
| `runway_days` | 7 | 0 or more | A campaign with this many days of leads left or fewer is flagged. |
| `max_leads_per_campaign` | 500 | 1 to 50,000 | The most leads to read per active campaign, for leads left. |
| `anomaly_threshold` | 2.5 | above 0 | Standard deviations from the baseline that make a day unusual. |
| `baseline_days` | 14 | 5 to 60 | Days before the period that form the anomaly baseline. |
| `min_baseline_points` | 5 | 2 or more, whole | The fewest baseline days with data a day needs to be tested. |
| `statuses`, `max_campaigns` | see above |  |  |

`data` (`ClientReport`):

- `days`, `period`, `previousPeriod`, `campaigns` (campaigns read), `campaignsRead` (the same number) and `moreCampaignsExist`.
- `replyRateBasis`: `"sent"`, or `"contacted"` when a period does not report messages sent. `totals`, `top` and `bottom` divide replies by it, as `periodComparison` does.
- `totals`: every campaign added together, as in `periodComparison`: `current`, `previous`, `change`, `replyRateBasis`, `replyRateTest`, `verdict` and `finding`. A rate counts only the campaigns that report both sides of it.
- `top`, `bottom`: the best and worst campaigns by the low end of the interval of the reply rate, over `replyRateBasis`. `bottom` never repeats a campaign in `top`.
- `bounceProblems`: campaigns over `max_bounce_rate`, worst first, with the rate as `count`, `of`, `rate`, `low` and `high`, and `bounced` and `sent`, the same counts under their older names.
- `anomalies`: unusual days in the period, workspace-wide. `null` when the adapter cannot serve them.
- `runningOutOfLeads`: active campaigns with `runway_days` of leads or fewer, with `remaining`, `dailyContactRate` and `daysLeft`, soonest first. A campaign with no leads left has `daysLeft` 0, so it comes first and gets an `add_leads` action. `null` when the adapter cannot serve them.
- `actions`: what to do, most pressing first. Each has a `kind` (`fix_bounces`, `add_leads`, `reply_rate_fell`, `review_campaign` or `check_anomaly`), the `campaign` and `campaignName` it is about, an `action` sentence and the `number` behind it.
- `leftOut`: the sections left out because the adapter cannot serve them.

- **Needs:** `stats.campaign`. Unusual days also need `stats.byDay` and workspace stats. Leads left also needs `leads.byCampaign` and `stats.byDay`.
- **Costs:** two stats requests per campaign, one for the workspace, and for each active campaign two more plus its pages of leads.
- **Notes it can add:** one per section left out, starting "Left out."; a rate of `totals` leaves out campaigns that do not report one side of it; a cap was reached.
- **Does not:** filter by tag or by campaign. It reports on everything the instance holds, or the one workspace it is scoped to. A campaign whose leads were cut off at `max_leads_per_campaign` is never flagged as running out, because its count is a minimum. It writes no email and sends nothing.
